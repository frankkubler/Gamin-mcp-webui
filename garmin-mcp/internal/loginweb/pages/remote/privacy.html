Who processes what

The operator of this deployment runs this server and holds its encryption key. They decide how long it runs and who may use it, and they are the people to contact about your data. This software does not send anything to its authors.

What is written to the database

Your e-mail address
In the clear. It is your login handle and the only human-readable identifier of your account. It is never used to isolate your data — that is done with a random internal identifier, so changing your e-mail moves nothing.
A fingerprint of your Garmin account identifier
A keyed digest, not the identifier itself. It exists so the same Garmin account cannot silently become two accounts here.
Your Garmin account identifier and display name
Encrypted, readable only with this deployment's key.
Your Garmin session tokens
Encrypted. They are what lets this server call Garmin on your behalf without asking for your password again. They are replaced whenever Garmin rotates them.
Your authorizations
Which client you allowed, for which address and which permissions, when you granted it and, if it happens, when it was revoked. A revocation is recorded rather than erased, so the trail of what was allowed survives.
Digests of the access tokens issued to your clients
The tokens themselves are never stored and cannot be recovered from what is. Their issue, expiry and rotation instants are stored, which is what lets a stolen token be detected and revoked.
This acceptance
A digest of the exact text you are reading, the label it carries, and the instant you accepted it. That is the record that you were shown this, and which version.

What is never written to the database

  • Your Garmin password. It is used once, to sign you in to Garmin, and is never stored, never logged, and never sent anywhere but Garmin.
  • Your health and activity data. No heart rate, sleep, weight, workout, training metric or GPS coordinate is stored here. The database has no column for any of it. When a client asks for such data, this server fetches it from Garmin and hands it to the client while the request runs; nothing is kept afterwards, on disk or in a cache.
  • Your IP address, your browser, your device. The database has no column for them. Your address is used while a request is in flight, to apply rate limits. Whatever the operator's own infrastructure records — a reverse proxy, a container log — is outside this software and is the operator's to describe.
  • What you ask your assistant. The content of your conversations with an MCP client never reaches this server.

What a client you authorize receives

An access token valid for this server alone, limited to the permissions shown on this page, and the data it then asks for. It never receives your Garmin password, your Garmin tokens, or access to anyone else's account. A token stops working when it expires, when you have its authorization revoked, or when this server detects that an old one was replayed.

How long it is kept

Your account, your authorizations and this acceptance stay until the operator removes them. Expired authorization codes and tokens are deleted automatically by the server's cleanup, after a short retention that exists so a replayed token can still be recognized. Encrypted Garmin tokens are deleted when your account is unlinked.

How to withdraw

Ask the operator to revoke your clients' authorizations, which stops every current token, and to unlink your Garmin account, which deletes the stored Garmin tokens and the link to your Garmin identity. Your acceptance record is kept as the evidence of what you were shown; it goes when the account itself is deleted.

Where this text comes from

It describes this build, and it is served from the software itself rather than from a page an operator can edit invisibly: the digest recorded with your acceptance is the digest of this text. If it changes, you will be asked again.
